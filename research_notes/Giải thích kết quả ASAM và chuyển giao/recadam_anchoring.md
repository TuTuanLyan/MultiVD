## Q1. The original RecAdam: objective, annealing function, hyperparameters, forgetting claim, GLUE gains, and where lambda(t) is applied

### Takeaway
RecAdam's loss is lambda(t)*Loss_T + (1 - lambda(t)) * (gamma/2) * sum_i (theta_i - theta*_i)^2 with a
sigmoid lambda(t), but the paper and code deliberately do NOT put lambda(t) into the gradient that Adam
normalizes: lambda(t) multiplies the already-normalized Adam step, and the pull is added outside Adam.
That makes lambda(t) mathematically a per-step learning-rate multiplier (a warmup schedule) on the
target-task update. The original recipe is also very different from the one in this project: gamma =
5000 (lr*gamma = 0.1), t0 in the hundreds to a thousand absolute steps (lambda(1) ~ 0), random
initialization pulled to the pretrained weights, and a task head trained by plain Adam with no lambda
scaling and no anchor. Reported gains: +1.0 average median GLUE (BERT-base), concentrated on small tasks;
no standard deviations or failure counts are reported, and "forgetting" is measured only as Euclidean
weight distance.

### Cited Findings
- Objective (Objective Shifting): "We replace the coefficient lambda [sym] in the optimization objective of multi-task learning (as shown in Eq. 1) with the annealing function lambda(t) [sym], where t refers to the update timesteps during fine-tuning." The loss is "Loss = lambda(t)LossT + (1 - lambda(t))LossS" [sym], with LossS the quadratic penalty from "Pretraining Simulation" (gamma/2 * sum (theta_i - theta*_i)^2, written out in the official optimizer's comment: "Loss = lambda(t)*Loss_T + (1-lambda(t))*\gamma/2*\sum((\theta_i-\theta_i^*)^2)") - [Chen et al., EMNLP 2020, PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf); [official RecAdam.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam/master/RecAdam.py)
- Annealing function: lambda(t) = 1 / (1 + exp(-k (t - t0))) [sym], "where k and t0 are the hyperparameters controlling the annealing rate and timesteps." Figure 1 caption: "Fine-tuning and multi-task learning can be regarded as the special cases (k -> infinity and k -> 0) of our method." [sym]. The code also offers `linear` (min(1, step/t0)*w) and `constant` - [Chen et al. PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf); [RecAdam.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam/master/RecAdam.py)
- Where lambda(t) is applied (decoupled from Adam): "We introduce Recall Adam (RECADAM) optimizer to integrate the quadratic penalty and the annealing coefficient ... by decoupling them from the gradient updates in Adam optimizer." "Otherwise, both the quadratic penalty and annealing coefficient would be adapted by the gradient update rules, resulting in different magnitudes of the quadratic penalty among the model's weights." "With RECADAM optimizer, we decouple the gradient of the quadratic penalty (theta - theta*) [sym] and the annealing coefficient lambda(t) [sym] in Line 12 of Algorithm 1." Algorithm 1 line 12 is theta_t <- theta_{t-1} - eta_t ( lambda(t) * alpha * m_hat_t / (sqrt(v_hat_t) + eps) + (1 - lambda(t)) * gamma * (theta_{t-1} - theta*) ) [sym]; the rejected "Adam" variant (line 6) instead feeds g_t <- lambda(t) grad f + (1 - lambda(t)) gamma (theta - theta*) into Adam - [Chen et al. PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf)
- Official code confirms the step form: `p.data.addcdiv_(-step_size * anneal_lambda, exp_avg, denom)` then `p.data.add_(-group["lr"] * (group['anneal_w'] - anneal_lambda) * group["pretrain_cof"], p.data - pp.data)`; optimizer defaults `anneal_k=0, anneal_t0=0, anneal_w=1.0, pretrain_cof=5000.0` - [RecAdam.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam/master/RecAdam.py). The pull uses `group["lr"]`, i.e. the scheduler-modified LR.
- The paper keeps warmup as a separate knob: "SetScheduleMultiplier(t) (Line 11) refers to the procedure (e.g. warm-up technique) to get the scaling factor of the step size." - [Chen et al. PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf)
- Hyperparameters (BERT-base): "we set the learning rate to 2e-5, use the gradient bias correction and select the training step (61,360 on MNLI, 56,855 on QQP, 33,890 on QNLI, 21,050 on SST, 13,400 on CoLA, 9,000 on STS, 11,500 on MRPC, 7,800 on RTE) to improve the fine-tuning stability on each task (Mosbach et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020b)." "we set gamma [sym] in the quadratic penalty to 5,000, and select the best t0 and k in {100, 250, 500, 1,000} and {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1} respectively for the annealing coefficient lambda(t) [sym] on each dev set." "we report the score of 5 differently-seeded runs for each result." - [Chen et al. PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf)
- README differs slightly: BERT-base t0 grid "{250, 500, 1,000}"; ALBERT "{100, 250, 500} for small tasks and {250, 500, 1,000} for large tasks"; both example commands use `--recadam_anneal_t0 1000 --recadam_anneal_k 0.1 --recadam_pretrain_cof 5000.0`; the BERT-base example has no `--warmup_steps` (script default `default=0`), the ALBERT example uses `--warmup_steps 320` - [official README](https://github.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam); [run_glue_with_RecAdam.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam/master/run_glue_with_RecAdam.py)
- Head and initialization: "We fine-tune the additional output layer with the vanilla Adam optimizer, since it is excluded in the parameters of pretrained LMs. Our methods use random initialization because of the pretrained knowledge recalling implementation, while vanilla fine-tuning initializes the fine-tuning model with the pretrained parameters." In the script, parameter groups whose names do not contain `args.model_type` (the classifier) get `"anneal_w": 0.0` (plain Adam: no lambda scaling, no pull); `--start_from_pretrain` is a store_true flag, otherwise `new_model = model_class(config=config)` (random init); script defaults `recadam_anneal_k=0.5`, `recadam_anneal_t0=250`, `recadam_pretrain_cof=5000.0` - [Chen et al. PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf); [run_glue_with_RecAdam.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam/master/run_glue_with_RecAdam.py)

- Forgetting claim and how it was measured: "We measure the pretrained knowledge forgetting by the Euclidean distance between the weights of the fine-tuning model and the pretrained model." - Internal: the project's `src/RecAdam.py` lines 119-128 are the same update (lambda on the normalized step, pull with `group["lr"]`); `src/train_transfer.py:1233` sets `anneal_t0 = max(1, int(args.anneal_t0_ratio * total_steps))`, i.e. t0 is a fraction of the run, not absolute steps; `build_recadam_groups` (`:979-1001`) gives the head group `"pretrain_cof": 0.0` but does not set `anneal_w = 0`, so even with `--recadam_anchor_head none` the head's Adam step is still multiplied by lambda(t), unlike the official script (local code, read-only).

### Inferences
- Because Adam normalizes by sqrt(v_hat), multiplying the LOSS by a slowly varying lambda(t) would leave the target-task step almost unchanged (m and sqrt(v) both scale by lambda). RecAdam's decoupled form is the opposite: it leaves Adam's statistics untouched and multiplies the step, so "objective shifting" is implemented as a learning-rate schedule on the target-task update plus a decoupled, L2-SP-like pull. In other words the paper's own Line 11/Line 12 split makes lambda(t) functionally a second warmup multiplier. (My reading of Algorithm 1 and the code.)
- In the original recipe, lambda(1) is essentially zero (k=0.05, t0=100 gives 7.0e-3; k=0.1, t0=1000 gives about 4e-44), lr*gamma = 0.1 pulls a randomly initialized backbone 10% per step onto theta*, and the head trains with full-rate plain Adam. For the first ~t0 steps this is "backbone held at theta*, head trained": a soft linear-probe-then-fine-tune (LP-FT) schedule followed by a sigmoid release over ~2/k steps. None of the three ingredients (lambda(1) ~ 0, strong pull, head exempt from lambda) is present in this project's configuration.
- In this project's configuration the anchor is weak and short. Sum over steps of (1 - lambda(t)) = 15.7 steps (about 1.05 epochs at 15 steps/epoch) of target-task progress is withheld, and the cumulative pull exponent sum lr*gamma*(1 - lambda(t)) = 0.157, so the anchor can remove at most about 15% (factor exp(-0.157) = 0.855) of the deviation that exists during the first ~3 epochs and essentially nothing afterwards. With a 10% linear LR warmup (45 steps) the pull also shrinks (it uses the scheduled LR): exponent 0.074 (at most ~7%), and lambda adds only 7.4 "lost" full-LR steps on top of the 22 lost to the warmup itself.
- The paper's evidence for "less forgetting" is distance in weight space plus downstream gains; it never measures retention of a pretraining capability, and reports median/max, not variance or failure counts. So the paper itself provides no evidence that RecAdam reduces collapse or across-seed variance.

### Gaps
- Whether the authors' BERT-base runs used any LR warmup is only inferable from the README example (no `--warmup_steps`, default 0); the paper text does not say.
- The paper does not report per-seed results, standard deviations, or failed-run counts; I found no RecAdam ablation separating "lambda(t) step scaling" from "quadratic pull".
- Exact Greek symbols in extracted equations were restored by matching with the code; the typeset PDF was not visually inspected.

## Q2. Later replications and comparisons (Zheng et al. ACL 2023; Xu et al. EMNLP 2021; other benchmarks), including stability across seeds

### Takeaway
Independent re-implementations find RecAdam gives small mean gains (+0.55 to +0.75 GLUE average on BERT-large, roughly the same as plain weight decay toward the pretrained weights) and, in the only table I found that reports standard deviations for RecAdam, a HIGHER across-seed std than vanilla fine-tuning on 6/7 datasets and a lower mean on 4/7. Neither replication supports RecAdam as a stabilizer. Older stability studies show that anchoring-style regularizers (pre-trained weight decay) lose their apparent stabilizing effect once Adam bias correction and longer training are used.

### Inferences
- Across three independent tables RecAdam's mean gain is within the spread of the other regularizers and roughly equal to plain weight decay toward the pretrained weights (Child-Tuning: +0.75 vs +0.67; Match-Tuning: +0.55 vs +0.45). Nothing in the replications suggests a benefit beyond what an L2-SP-type pull provides.
- The Zheng table is the closest thing to a direct "does RecAdam reduce variance" test, and the answer is no (6/7 higher std). Caveat: 5 runs per cell make each std estimate noisy, and most other regularizers also show higher std than vanilla, so the defensible statement is "no evidence that RecAdam reduces seed variance", not "RecAdam increases variance".
- Zhang et al. and Mixout together say that the classic claim "anchoring stabilizes fine-tuning" was largely an artifact of BERTAdam without bias correction and short training; this project uses bias correction (`correct_bias=True` is the constructor default in `src/RecAdam.py` and `src/train_transfer.py` does not override it), so the literature predicts little stabilizing value from the anchor itself.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that reports failed-run / collapse counts for RecAdam specifically (only means, max, or std).
- Zheng et al. use RAdam with a constant schedule as the base optimizer; how they combined that with RecAdam (whose code is Adam-based) is not stated in the parts I read, so their RecAdam numbers may come from a different base optimizer than the original.
- Match-Tuning's IJCAI 2022 venue was not re-verified this session.

## Q3. L2-SP (Li, Grandvalet, Davoine, ICML 2018), Gouk et al. distance-based regularization, and whether anchoring helps when source and target differ

### Takeaway
L2-SP (a constant, never-annealed pull toward the starting point) consistently beat plain L2 in vision transfer, including when the source was the "wrong" database, and its Fisher-weighted variant did not help the target task. Gouk et al. show that a penalty does not actually bound how far weights move, and that a hard constraint enforced throughout training works better. Both point the same way for this project: an anchor that is weak and disappears after ~3 epochs is the least effective form in this family. There is also evidence (DTNH) that an anchor to an inappropriate source can conflict with the target gradient and hurt.

### Cited Findings
- L2-SP results, 5 runs, mean +/- std (L2 -> L2-SP -> L2-SP-Fisher): MIT Indoors 67 79.6+/-0.5 -> 84.2+/-0.3 -> 84.0+/-0.4; Stanford Dogs 120 81.4+/-0.2 -> 85.1+/-0.2 -> 85.1+/-0.2; Caltech 256-30 81.5+/-0.2 -> 83.5+/-0.1 -> 83.3+/-0.1; Caltech 256-60 85.3+/-0.2 -> 86.4+/-0.2 -> 86.0+/-0.1. "L2-SP and L2-SP-Fisher always improve over L2, and that when less training data are available for the target problem, the improvement of L2-SP and L2-SP-Fisher compared to L2 are more important." - [Li, Grandvalet, Davoine, arXiv:1802.01483 (ICML 2018, PMLR 80)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.01483)
- L2-SP with mismatched source: "As expected, the results of transfer learning are much better when source and target are alike ... It is however interesting to note that the trends are similar for the two source databases: all the fine-tuning strategies based on penalties using the starting point -SP as a reference perform consistently better than standard fine-tuning (L2). There is thus a benefit in having an explicit bias towards the starting point, even when the target task is not too similar to the source task." - [Li et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.01483)
- L2-SP-Fisher does not help the target: "the variants using the Fisher information matrix behave like the simpler variants using a Euclidean metric on parameters. We believe that this is due to the fact that, contrary to lifelong learning, our objective does not favor solutions that retain accuracy on the source task." - [Li et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.01483)
- Gouk, Hospedales, Pontil (ICLR 2021): the algorithm "constrains the hypothesis class to a small sphere centred on the initial pre-trained weights" and "outperforms both state of the art fine-tuning competitors, and penalty-based alternatives that we show do not directly constrain the radius of the search space." Results: "the projection-based methods exhibit a significant increase in accuracy over their penalty counterparts that use the same distance metrics. This suggests that, in the case of fine-tuning, using a projection to enforce a constraint on the weights throughout the training process is a better regularisation strategy than adding a term to the objective function that penalises the deviations of weights from their pre-trained values." Conclusion: "enforcing a hard constraint throughout the entire training process on the distances the parameters can move is far more effective than the widely used strategy of adding a penalty term to the objective function." 5 seeds, vision only - [Gouk et al., arXiv:2002.08253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08253)
- Wan et al. (DTNH, ICDM 2019): "transferring weights from inappropriate networks hurts training procedure and may lead to even lower accuracy"; they treat it as the regularizer (L2-SP, knowledge distillation) restricting loss minimization through conflicting descent directions, and report "0.1%--7% higher accuracy" from de-conflicting the two gradients - [Wan et al., arXiv:1911.07489](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07489) (abstract only)
- Mosbach et al. (ICLR 2021) on whether forgetting drives instability: "catastrophic forgetting affects only the top layers of the network in our experiments often around [footnote text removed] 10 out of 24 layers, and the same is however also true for the successfully fine-tuned models, except for a much smaller increase in perplexity"; "This suggests that the observed fine-tuning failure is rather an optimization problem causing catastrophic forgetting in the top layers of the pre-trained model." - [Mosbach et al., arXiv:2006.04884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04884)
- Internal: in this project the Phase-1 checkpoint scored about 0.52 when evaluated zero-shot on the Python target (alpha = 0 of the INT1 interpolation), and anchor strength in gamma 0.5 to 5000 made no difference above the 0.010 noise floor in OPT1 (`RESEARCH_2026-09-06_recadam.md` sections 10.1, 12.1; internal, not literature).

### Inferences
- L2-SP's positive result is for a pull that stays on for the whole run (and in SGD, not Adam). RecAdam as configured here is the opposite on both axes Gouk identifies as important (penalty rather than constraint; active only during ~48 of ~450 steps). The family's best-supported form is therefore not what is being tested.
- "Anchoring helps even when source and target differ" (L2-SP) concerns ImageNet vs Places365 sources for natural-image targets, which still share low-level features. The project's Phase-1 checkpoint has near-chance zero-shot behaviour on the target, which is closer to DTNH's "inappropriate network" case, where pulling toward the source can conflict with the target gradient.
- The Fisher null in L2-SP and the target-not-source objective argument match this project's own finding that anchoring adds nothing measurable to target accuracy.

### Gaps
- No paper I found evaluates L2-SP or RecAdam on a sequential source-task-then-target-task setup where the anchor is an intermediate fine-tuned checkpoint (rather than the pretrained model), which is this project's setting.
- DTNH was read only at the abstract level.

## Q4. LP-FT and feature distortion (Kumar et al. ICLR 2022), task-head preparation (Ren et al. ICLR 2023): does early anchoring act like LP-FT?

### Takeaway
LP-FT works because the head is already good when the backbone starts moving, so the large early gradients that a random or misaligned head sends into the backbone never happen. Early anchoring is only LP-FT-like if the head is free to learn while the backbone is held. The original RecAdam does this (head exempt from lambda and from the pull, backbone held by lambda ~ 0 plus a strong pull); this project's configuration does not (lambda scales head and backbone equally, lambda(1) = 0.46, and the pull is weak). Kumar et al. also found that an explicit L2-SP-type pull closes only part of the LP-FT gap.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: "We prove that the OOD error of fine-tuning is high when we initialize with a fixed or random head" ... "this is because while fine-tuning learns the head, the lower layers of the neural network change simultaneously and distort the pretrained features." "Our theory shows that fine-tuning underpeforms [sic] because when trying to fit ID training data with a randomly initialized head, the feature extractor changes significantly for ID examples, making features for ID and OOD examples largely inconsistent." - [Kumar et al., arXiv:2202.10054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10054)
- Early stopping does not fix it: "this feature distortion issue cannot be simply fixed by early stopping" and, empirically, "fine-tuning indeed never matches the OOD accuracy of linear probing throughout the course of training (if the pretrained features are good, and OOD shift is large)"; "LP-FT indeed changes both ID and OOD features 10-100x less than fine-tuning does" [x restored]; fine-tuning can beat linear probing OOD "if the pretrained features are not very high quality" - [Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10054)
- Headline: "LP-FT outperforms both fine-tuning and linear probing on the above datasets (1% better ID, 10% better OOD than full fine-tuning)." - [Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10054)
- Explicit anchoring vs LP-FT (Appendix B.4, Living-17): they test a 10x head learning rate and "regularize the Euclidean distance between the current feature extractor weights (so ignoring the linear head) from the pretrained weights", noting "method 2 corresponds to L2-SP in (Li et al., 2018)". Result: "both (1) and (2) are able to close part of the OOD gap between fine-tuning and linear-probing. However, LP-FT does better than both methods ID and OOD." - [Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10054)
- Ren et al. (ICLR 2023): "the key aspect is the training accuracy and loss at the beginning of finetuning, which determines the energy available for the features adaptation ... as the energy increases, the Euclidean and cosine distances between the resulting and original features increase, while their dot products (and the resulting features norm) first increase then decrease." Practical advice: stop head probing early ("we cannot select based on the standard early stopping criterion on HP train accuracy"), and "If we want a mild adaptation, but the training accuracy during HP goes to 100% too fast, using label smoothing during HP can be considered." - [Ren et al., arXiv:2302.05779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05779)
- Tomihari & Sato (NeurIPS 2024), LP-FT for Transformers/NLP via NTK: "This decomposition highlights the importance of the linear head norm alongside the prediction accuracy at the start of the FT stage. We also observe a significant increase in the linear head norm during LP ... This increase in the linear head norm effectively reduces changes in learned features." - [Tomihari & Sato, arXiv:2405.16747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16747) (abstract only)
- Original RecAdam already has the LP-FT ingredients (head on plain Adam via `"anneal_w": 0.0`; backbone held by lambda ~ 0 and lr*gamma = 0.1) - see Q1 citations to [run_glue_with_RecAdam.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sanyuan-Chen/RecAdam/master/run_glue_with_RecAdam.py) and the [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf).

### Inferences
- In two-phase transfer the Phase-2 head is inherited from Phase 1, not random, but the project's own INT1 measurement (zero-shot ~0.52 on the target) says it is functionally misaligned: in Ren et al.'s terms the initial training loss is near ln 2 and the "energy" for feature change is high. That is exactly the regime where LP-FT-type protection matters.
- With lambda multiplying head and backbone steps equally, the ratio of head movement to backbone movement is unchanged from AdamW, so RecAdam here cannot act like LP-FT; it only slows both. It does not change the "head and backbone move simultaneously" condition that Kumar et al. identify as the cause.
- A version that would act like LP-FT is: head group with anneal_w = 0 (plain Adam), backbone lambda(1) close to 0 for a short window. That is the original RecAdam recipe, not a new method, and it is not what the current `--recadam_anchor_head none` flag does (it removes the head's pull but keeps the head's lambda scaling).
- Kumar et al.'s Appendix B.4 suggests that even when it is set up correctly, an L2-SP-type pull is weaker than an actual probe-then-fine-tune.

### Gaps
- Kumar et al.'s theory and experiments are about OOD robustness in vision (and CLIP); the link from "feature distortion" to "training collapse to a constant predictor" is my inference, not something these papers test.
- I found no paper that measures whether LP-FT or head warm-starting reduces the rate of failed/collapsed fine-tuning runs for code models.

## Q5. Warmup as a stabilizer (Mosbach ICLR 2021; RAdam ICLR 2020; Kalra & Barkeshli and Kosson et al. NeurIPS 2024): is RecAdam's benefit just warmup?

### Takeaway
There is a direct precedent for reading a multiplicative early step-size factor as warmup: Mosbach et al. describe Adam's bias correction exactly that way and link its absence to failed BERT runs whose training loss stays constant. Mechanistic warmup papers attribute warmup's benefit to preventing early instabilities (adaptive-LR variance, high preconditioned sharpness, oversized or angular updates) that can end in training failure. RecAdam's lambda(t) is the same kind of factor (0.46 -> 0.9 over 48 steps here), and its size matches the ~1 epoch delay that was measured, so warmup is the most parsimonious explanation for the delay. Whether it also explains the lower collapse count is unproven: the same collapse still happened with an explicit 10% warmup.

### Cited Findings
- Mosbach et al., failure mode: "the observed instability is caused by optimization difficulties that lead to vanishing gradients." "Failed fine-tuning runs suffer from vanishing gradients. We observed in Fig. 2c that the failed runs have practically constant training loss throughout the training". Figure 2 caption: "Failed fine-tuning runs lead to a trivial training loss suggesting an optimization problem." Failed run definition (following Dodge et al.): accuracy at end of training "less or equal to that of a majority classifier" - [Mosbach et al., arXiv:2006.04884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04884)
- Mosbach et al., bias correction is implicit warmup: "Kingma and Ba (2015) already describe the effect of the bias correction as to reduce the learning rate at the beginning of training." The correction "boils down to reducing the original step size by a multiplicative factor sqrt(1 - beta2^t)/(1 - beta1^t) [sym] which is significantly below 1 for the first iterations of training and approaches 1 as the number of training iterations t increases". "You et al. (2020) explicitly remark that bias correction in ADAM has a similar effect to the warmup which is widely used in deep learning to prevent divergence early in training ... The implicit warmup of ADAM is likely to be an important factor that contributed to its success." - [Mosbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04884)
- Mosbach et al., recommendation: "Use small learning rates with bias correction to avoid vanishing gradients early in training. Increase the number of iterations considerably and train to (almost) zero training loss." Their baseline uses LR 2e-5, 20 epochs, linear warmup over the first 10% - [Mosbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04884)
- Liu et al. (RAdam, ICLR 2020): "the adaptive learning rate has undesirably large variance in the early stage of model training, due to the limited amount of training samples being used. Thus, to reduce such variance, it is better to use smaller learning rates in the first few epochs of training, which justifies the warmup heuristic." Without warmup "the gradient distribution is distorted to have a mass center in relatively small values within 10 updates. Such gradient distortion means that the vanilla Adam is trapped in bad/suspicious local optima after the first few" updates - [Liu et al., arXiv:1908.03265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.03265)
- Kalra & Barkeshli (NeurIPS 2024): "the overwhelming benefit of warmup arises from allowing the network to tolerate larger eta_trgt [sym] by forcing the network to more well-conditioned areas of the loss landscape." For Adam, "the pre-conditioned sharpness typically starts off at high values (even in the large batch case), causing considerable instabilities at high learning rates. Such instabilities, which may be retained in Adam's memory, can result in performance degradation and even training failures. Warmup mitigates such instabilities by gradually pushing down the preconditioned sharpness, enhancing performance, and preventing training failures." Also: "We further find that the performance of the network is largely determined by the target learning rate." - [Kalra & Barkeshli, arXiv:2406.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09405); venue at [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/ca98452d4e9ecbc18c40da2aa0da8b98-Abstract.html)
- Kosson, Messmer, Jaggi (NeurIPS 2024): "we argue that warmup benefits training by keeping the overall size of Delta w_t [sym] limited, counteracting large initial values of u_t" and "warmup helps counteract large angular updates as well as a limited critical batch size early in training"; the need for warmup "can be significantly reduced or eliminated by modifying the optimizer to explicitly normalize u_t" (small-scale GPT, AdamW/Lion) - [Kosson et al., arXiv:2410.23922](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23922); venue at [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/059445c2d5b3ef918079851628fef1d6-Abstract.html)
- Zhang et al. (ICLR 2021): stabilizers that only "ease the optimization difficulty brought by the debiasing omission" lose effect once debiasing is added (quoted in Q2) - [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05987)
- RecAdam authors acknowledge the slow-start effect of small k (quoted in Q1: "As k decreases, it takes a longer time for the model to converge on the target task") - [Chen et al. PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf)
- Internal: an earlier project control, `warm` (AdamW with the same lambda(t) applied to the LR and no anchor), vs `plain` AdamW gave -0.0008 F1, 3/6 folds (verification tier, n=3 folds, no ASAM, accuracy only), recorded in `RESEARCH_2026-09-06_recadam.md` section 7.3; the flag `--adamw_anneal_lr` in `src/train_transfer.py` implements this control.

### Inferences
- Quantitatively, lambda(t) withholds 15.7 steps of target-task progress (about 1.05 epochs at 15 steps/epoch). That matches the measured ~1 epoch delay in reaching val ROC 0.85, so the delay needs no other explanation.
- The measured "ROC +0.001 over ASAM-only" is also what Kalra & Barkeshli predict for a warmup at a fixed, already-tolerable target LR (performance set mainly by the target LR; a longer warmup adds little).
- For collapse, the warmup literature gives a plausible mechanism (early Adam instability at high preconditioned sharpness can end in a failed run; a smaller early step avoids it), and Mosbach's failed runs (near-constant training loss at a trivial value) look like this project's collapsed cells. But two facts argue against "it is all warmup": (a) with an explicit 10% LR warmup, RecAdam+ASAM still collapsed in some folds, and in that block lambda adds only ~7 extra lost steps on top of 22 from the warmup; (b) collapse that persists for 30 epochs is a late-phase trapping phenomenon, which warmup theory does not address (see Q6).
- The decisive control already exists in the codebase (`--adamw_anneal_lr`: same lambda(t) on the LR, no anchor), but it was run without ASAM and only for accuracy. Under CLAUDE.md section 9 this would need the user's approval before being queued; I note it only as the test that would separate the two mechanisms.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that explicitly analyzes RecAdam's lambda(t) as a warmup; the earlier project note already flagged this as an original observation (`RESEARCH_2026-08-20_0959.md` section 3). The Mosbach bias-correction passage is the closest published precedent for the argument.
- No warmup paper I read studies small-data fine-tuning of code models specifically.

## Q6. Interaction of SAM with warmup, and of SAM with anchoring regularizers

### Takeaway
There is theory that SAM can turn saddle points into attractors and escapes them worse than gradient descent, which fits a long ASAM-induced plateau that an early, short anchor/warmup cannot shorten. There is weak empirical evidence (single runs, ViT) that applying SAM perturbations during warmup is slightly harmful and a published design (GSAM) that ties rho to the LR schedule. I found no paper that studies SAM combined with an anchoring regularizer such as L2-SP or RecAdam.

### Cited Findings
- Kim, Park, Choi, Lee, "Stability Analysis of Sharpness-Aware Minimization" (arXiv:2301.06308, v2 June 2026, "Accepted to ICML 2026"): "we explain how SAM becomes stuck in the saddle point and theoretically prove that the saddle point can become an attractor under SAM dynamics." "We prove that SAM diffusion is worse than that of vanilla gradient descent in terms of saddle point escape." "often overlooked training tricks, momentum and batch-size, might be important to mitigate the convergence instability and achieve high generalization performance." - [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06308); [ICML 2026 poster page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61132)
- Becker, Altrock, Risse (Momentum-SAM, arXiv:2401.12033, v3 Oct 2025, venue not stated): "we do not apply MSAM during the warmup phase by default (i.e. setting rho = 0) [sym] since if doing so, we observe a drop in test accuracy from 69.1% to 66.1% which is below the AdamW baseline." "A similar effect can be seen for SAM, however, it is less pronounced, so that applying perturbations during the warm-up phase does not thwart SAM hugely." "we would generally propose to apply SAM only after the warmup phase for ViT models to further improve SAM." Table A.2 (ViT-S/32, ImageNet): AdamW 67.1, SAM 69.2, SAM after warmup only 69.8, MSAM 69.1, MSAM during warmup 66.1; "we only run single iterations". They also say Zhuang et al. bound rho to the LR schedule "and they did not notice benefits" - [Becker et al., arXiv:2401.12033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12033)
- Zhuang et al. (GSAM, ICLR 2022): Algorithm 1 uses a rho schedule following the LR, rho_t = rho_min + (rho_max - rho_min)(lr_t - lr_min)/(lr_max - lr_min) [sym], and the convergence theorem assumes "perturbation amplitude rho_t [sym] proportional to the learning rate" - [Zhuang et al., arXiv:2203.08065](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.08065)
- Zhou et al. (ICLR 2025): "even a few epochs of SAM applied at the end of training yield nearly the same generalization and solution sharpness as full SAM training" (from search-result summary of the abstract) - [arXiv:2410.10373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10373); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/35d5ad984cc0ddd84c6f1c177a2066e5-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Mosbach et al.: failed runs show "practically constant training loss throughout the training" (Q5) - [Mosbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04884)
- Internal: the project's `src/sam.py` docstring records that on 27/08 ASAM with rho = 0.05 left codebert at train loss = ln 2 for 13 epochs (local code comment, read-only).

### Inferences
- If ASAM collapse is "trapped at a saddle/plateau near the starting point", then a pull toward theta* (the starting point) cannot help escape it and might hold the model nearer to it while active; and since the RecAdam pull is gone by about step 48, it cannot affect a plateau that lasts 8-13 epochs. That matches the measured "plateau not shortened" (8 shorter / 9 longer / 13 equal paired cells).
- What RecAdam can change is the first ~3 epochs of the trajectory: smaller descent steps (lambda < 1) while ASAM's ascent radius is unchanged. In the project's code the SAM ascent radius is a fixed rho (per the `src/sam.py` docstring) and only the descent step is scaled by lambda, so early on the ratio of perturbation to descent step is larger than under AdamW+ASAM. Whether that steers the run away from or toward the trap is not predicted by any paper I found; Momentum-SAM's single-run ViT result weakly suggests SAM perturbations during a warmup are mildly harmful, which would cut the other way.
- A single rescued cell (0.916 vs stuck for 30 epochs) is consistent with "any perturbation of the early trajectory changes which basin is entered", which Kim et al.'s attractor picture also implies; on its own (n=1) it does not distinguish a systematic stabilizer from trajectory chaos.

### Gaps
- No paper found on SAM/ASAM combined with an anchoring regularizer (L2-SP, RecAdam, EWC), or on SAM collapse in small-data fine-tuning of pretrained transformers. TRAM (ICLR 2024, SAM with a trust region on representations) is the nearest relative; it is recorded in `RESEARCH_2026-09-06_recadam.md` section 2.2 but was not re-read this session.
- Zhou et al. ICLR 2025 was seen only through search-result text, not the PDF.

## Q8. Mechanism-by-mechanism assessment against the measured pattern

### Takeaway
The measured pattern (no accuracy gain, ~1 epoch delay, plateau not shortened, fewer permanent collapses 0/45 vs 3/35 with p ~ 0.08, collapse still present with an explicit 10% warmup) is best explained by "RecAdam here = AdamW plus a ~48-step implicit warmup plus a pull that removes at most ~15% of early drift". The forgetting/knowledge-retention mechanism RecAdam was designed for, and an LP-FT-like effect, are not active in this configuration. A causal claim that RecAdam prevents collapse is not supported by the literature and is weak in the data.

### Cited Findings
- Measured pattern as given in the brief (internal): ROC +0.001 vs ASAM-only; ~1 epoch later to reach val ROC 0.85; plateau shorter 8 / longer 9 / equal 13 paired cells; permanent collapse 0/45 (RecAdam+ASAM) vs 3/35 (ASAM only), p ~ 0.08; one cell 0.916 vs stuck 30 epochs; collapse still present in some folds of the block with LR warmup 0.1.
- RecAdam's decoupled lambda(t) multiplies the normalized step (Q1) - [Chen et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.634.pdf); bias correction as a multiplicative early step factor is described as implicit warmup (Q5) - [Mosbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04884)
- Anchoring regularizers lose their stabilizing effect once Adam is debiased - [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05987); pre-trained weight decay left degenerate runs in place - [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11299); RecAdam std above vanilla on 6/7 datasets - [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10790)
- Penalties do not bound the radius; constraints enforced throughout training work better - [Gouk et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08253)
- SAM saddle points can become attractors; SAM escapes saddles worse than GD - [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06308)

### Inferences
- (a) Knowledge retention / less forgetting (RecAdam's stated mechanism). Inconsistent with the pattern. The pull is active for ~48 of ~450 steps and its cumulative effect is at most a ~15% shrink of early deviation (~7% with LR warmup); target ROC does not move (+0.001); the project's own OPT1/RET1/INT1 blocks found no measurable value of the anchor or of the source weights beyond initialization (`RESEARCH_2026-09-06_recadam.md` section 12.2, internal); Mosbach et al. show forgetting is not what causes failed fine-tuning runs. Verdict: not the explanation.
- (b) Implicit warmup through lambda(t). Consistent with the delay (15.7 withheld steps, about 1.05 epochs, matches ~1 epoch) and with "no accuracy gain" (Kalra & Barkeshli: at a fixed target LR, warmup length changes little). Plausible for the lower collapse count, because the warmup literature ties early Adam instability to training failure. Weakened by: collapse persisting under an explicit 10% warmup, where lambda adds only ~7 extra lost steps; and the earlier `warm` control showing no accuracy difference from AdamW (no ASAM, n=3 folds). Verdict: most parsimonious account of the delay; an unproven account of collapse. The control that separates it from the anchor is `--adamw_anneal_lr` with ASAM (would need user approval to queue).
- (c) LP-FT-like protection of features from a misaligned head. Not active: lambda scales the head and backbone equally (also under `--recadam_anchor_head none`, which only zeroes the head's pull), lambda(1) = 0.46, and the pull is weak. The original RecAdam recipe would provide it (head on plain Adam, lambda(1) ~ 0, lr*gamma = 0.1). Verdict: absent in this implementation; would need the head exempted from lambda.
- (d) Anchor as a brake that keeps ASAM out of degenerate regions or helps it escape a plateau. Inconsistent with "plateau not shortened" (8/9/13): the pull is gone before the plateaus that last many epochs, and pulling toward the starting point cannot help escape a saddle located near that starting point. Verdict: cannot explain late behaviour; at most it alters the first ~3 epochs.
- (e) Generic variance reduction / regularization. No literature support (Zheng: higher std on 6/7; Zhang: effect vanishes with debiasing; Mixout paper: weight decay toward the pretrained weights still yields degenerate runs). Verdict: not supported.
- (f) Trajectory perturbation (any change to early step sizes changes which basin a seed enters). Consistent with the single rescued cell and with a non-significant count difference; predicts that the "rescue" would not replicate cell-by-cell under another seed or GPU (the project's measured cross-machine noise floor is 0.010-0.028). Verdict: cannot be excluded at the current n.
- Statistics of the collapse count: for 0/45 vs 3/35, Fisher's exact test gives P(all 3 collapses fall in the ASAM-only group) = C(35,3)/C(80,3) = 6545/82160 = 0.080, so p ~ 0.08 is reproduced; the cells share folds, backbones and sources, so they are not independent and this p is optimistic.
