# SAM/ASAM with a large radius from step 1: why training can sit on the ln2 plateau, and what the literature proposes

Scope: theory of SAM near saddles and plateaus, the role of gradient normalization and rho size, practical fixes (delayed SAM, rho scheduling, renormalization), reports of failure in fine-tuning, the ASAM paper's own settings, and how each fits the measured MultiVD pattern (ASAM rho=0.5, eta=0.01, CodeBERT-based checkpoint, 456 Python functions, linear LR decay over 30 epochs, early stopping patience 8).

Method: every paper below was downloaded from arXiv as PDF and converted to text (pdftotext); quotes were copied from that text. Venue metadata was checked on arXiv abstract pages, PMLR / JMLR pages, and icml.cc poster pages where noted. ## Q1. Kim, Park, Choi, Lee: do saddle points become attractors under SAM, does SAM escape saddles worse than GD, and what do momentum and batch size do?

### Takeaway
Yes, with conditions. For the unnormalized step w^p = w + rho*grad, an index-one saddle is an attractor when rho > -1/lambda_1, and the authors state this "can be easily satisfied" for standard normalized SAM because the effective radius rho/||grad|| becomes very large near the saddle. In their diffusion model SAM's displacement away from a saddle is smaller than SGD's by a term proportional to rho (so SAM never escapes faster), and larger momentum or smaller batch speeds escape. Status: VERIFIED (arXiv v2 read in full; ICML 2026 confirmed).

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: Hoki Kim, Jinseong Park, Yujin Choi, Jaewook Lee, "Stability Analysis of Sharpness-Aware Minimization", Proceedings of the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning, Seoul, PMLR 306, 2026 (stated in the v2 PDF header). arXiv:2301.06308 (v1 16 Jan 2023; v2 1 Jun 2026, arXiv comment "Accepted to ICML 2026"). No publisher DOI (PMLR issues none). Status VERIFIED - [arXiv v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06308); poster page title confirmed at [icml.cc 2026 poster 61132](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61132)
- Abstract: "we explain how SAM becomes stuck in the saddle point and theoretically prove that the saddle point can become an attractor under SAM dynamics. Additionally, we show that this convergence instability can also occur in stochastic dynamical systems by establishing the diffusion of SAM. We prove that SAM diffusion is worse than that of vanilla gradient descent in terms of saddle point escape. Finally, we demonstrate that often overlooked training tricks, momentum and batch-size, might be important to mitigate the convergence instability and achieve high generalization performance." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Which SAM is analysed: eq. (5) is the unnormalized step w_t^p = w_t + rho*grad l(w_t). Quote: "Note that ρ can be normalized with the gradient norm as introduced in (Foret et al., 2020), i.e., ρ/∥∇ℓ(w)∥; however, a constant ρ in (5) shows similar or higher performance than the normalized version (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2022)." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Theorem 1 (exact): "Let d be an index-one saddle point of system (7) with a negative eigenvalue λ1 of the Hessian matrix Hℓ(d) of the loss function ℓ. Then, the saddle point d is an attractor of SAM dynamics in (5) if ρ > −1/λ1." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)

Moreover, this condition can be easily satisfied since the normalized radius ρ̃ = ρ/∥∇ℓ(w)∥ in the standard SAM dynamics (Foret et al., 2020), which becomes very large near the saddle point." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Toy demonstration: on f(x,y) = x^2 - y^2 started at (-3, -0.01), GD escapes while "SAM becomes stuck in the saddle point [...] eigenvalues of the Hessian at (0, 0) are λ = {2, −2} and thus λ + ρλ^2 > 0 for all eigenvalues"; Figure 5 uses rho = 1.0. - Basin-boundary mechanism (Case-III): "SAM can be hindered by the saddle point d when the perturbed weight wp falls into a different basin of attraction across a basin boundary (Case-III), whereas GD smoothly directs to the stable equilibrium point. Furthermore, if wp is consistently located in different basins, wt will not be able to escape the saddle point." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Corollary 1 (SAM escapes saddle point more slowly than SGD): Delta_SGD - Delta_SAM = (2*eta*t^2*|lambda_j|^3 / B)*rho + O(B^-1 eta t^3 lambda_j^4), valid "as |λj|t ≪ 1 near ill-conditioned saddle points"; text: "As (2ηt^2|λj|^3/B)ρ is always positive, the result implies that SAM escapes saddle points more slowly than SGD." Theorem 2 assumes the Fisher approximation of gradient-noise covariance and a second-order Taylor model near the saddle. - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Theorem 3 (momentum gamma, batch B), conclusion (exact): "Therefore, ∆SAM increases as (1) momentum γ increases and/or (2) batch size B decreases. Furthermore, under SAM, maintaining the same diffusion behavior as in standard SGD requires a larger γ, since ∆SAM decreases as ρ increases." Also: "Theorem 3 implies that increasing momentum γ and decreasing batch size B can reduce the time to escape a saddle point." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Toy network (Ziyin et al. setup, init near saddle): "the converged parameters of SAM are mostly saturated in the saddle point area, whereas SGD successfully converges to the global minima." For varying rho (normalized radius in that figure): "As ρ increases, the average loss of SAM increases. This result aligns with Corollary 1, as a larger ρ makes SAM more difficult to escape the saddle point in terms of diffusion." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- CIFAR-10 / ResNet-18, Table 1 (BN and augmentation on, 3 seeds, normalized radius): at rho = 0.5 test accuracy is 86.20±1.66 (momentum 0.0), 88.76±1.14 (0.9), 91.98±0.03 (0.95), versus 95.08±0.07 at rho = 0.1 with momentum 0.95; the max-min gap across momentum grows with rho: 1.22 (0.01), 1.45 (0.05), 2.80 (0.1), 5.79 (0.5). Quote: "as ρ increases, the gap between the best and worst performances increases". - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2) (PARTIAL only for column alignment of the table as extracted; the gaps recompute correctly from the cell values)
- With BN and augmentation off (rho = 0.1, B = 512 default): "The momentum improves the accuracy of SGD by approximately 5%, while it enhances the accuracy of SAM by more than 20%." - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)
- Positioning (Limitations): "Additionally, Chen et al. (2023) argued that the generalization benefit of SAM may stem from transient attraction to saddle points. ### Inferences
- Substituting the normalized radius rho/||g|| into Kim's condition lambda + rho*lambda^2 > 0 with lambda = -mu gives -mu + rho*mu^2/||g|| > 0, i.e. ||g|| < rho*mu. This is exactly the user's 1-D condition (see Q3).
- Kim's momentum result is for heavy-ball SGD; AdamW/RecAdam carry beta1 momentum but also per-coordinate rescaling, which the theory does not model. "More momentum helps" is therefore a hypothesis for this project, not a derived result.
- Kim's Table 1 places rho = 0.5 (normalized SAM) in the most fragile regime even for CIFAR-10 trained from scratch, where it lost 3 to 9 points against rho = 0.1.

### Gaps
- Kim et al. do not analyse ASAM, Adam-type base optimizers, fine-tuning from a pretrained checkpoint, or a cross-entropy plateau at ln2. Appendix A proofs were not checked line by line.
- The "Chen et al. (2023)" transient-attraction paper cited by Kim was not identified or read.

## Q2. Monzio Compagnoni et al. (ICML 2023): exact statement and conditions for "SAM is attracted to saddle points", and normalized SAM vs USAM

### Takeaway
They prove USAM (no normalization) converges to a saddle or even a maximum when rho > -1/lambda* (lambda* = the largest negative eigenvalue), and that for normalized SAM on a quadratic the origin is locally asymptotically stable whenever ||H X_t|| (the gradient norm) <= -rho*lambda*. They also show SAM's implicit regularizer is rho*E||grad||, which for large rho biases the optimizer "toward any point with zero gradients, including saddles and maxima". Unlike USAM, mini-batch SAM "eventually slowly escapes" thanks to noise. Status: VERIFIED.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: Enea Monzio Compagnoni, Luca Biggio, Antonio Orvieto, Frank Norbert Proske, Hans Kersting, Aurelien Lucchi, "An SDE for Modeling SAM: Theory and Insights", Proceedings of the 40th ICML, PMLR 202:25209-25253, 2023. arXiv:2301.08203 (v3, 4 Jun 2023). No publisher DOI. Status VERIFIED - [PMLR page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/monzio-compagnoni23a.html); [arXiv v3 PDF read](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Abstract: "Finally, we prove that SAM is attracted to saddle points under some realistic conditions." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Definitions: SAM is x_{k+1} = x_k - eta*grad f(x_k + rho*grad f/||grad f||) (eq. 4); USAM, introduced by Andriushchenko & Flammarion, "drops the gradient normalization in Eq. (4)"; DNSAM is an intermediate that normalizes by the full-batch gradient norm, used "for theoretical analyses", not as a practical optimizer. - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Setup for saddle theory: quadratic f(x) = (1/2) x^T H x; "If there are negative eigenvalues, we denote by λ∗ the largest negative eigenvalue." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- USAM ODE: dX_t = -H(I + rho*H) X_t dt, so X_t^j = X_0^j exp(-lambda_j(1 + rho*lambda_j)t). Lemma 4.1 (informal statement of Lemma C.2): "Let H have at least one strictly negative eigenvalue. Then, for all ρ > −1/λ∗, X_t^j → 0, ∀j". Followed by: "Therefore, if ρ is not chosen appropriately, USAM might converge to 0 ∈ R^d, even if it is a saddle point or a maximum, which is very undesirable." and "we also notice that since e^{−λj(1+ρλj)t} < e^{−λj t}, USAM will escape the saddle but more slowly than the gradient flow." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- USAM SDE (Theorem 4.2 / C.4): if rho > -1/lambda*, the Gaussian stationary distribution still exists for an indefinite H, i.e. the process "will oscillate around the origin even if this is a saddle or a maximum"; for small rho it escapes but "the escape from the saddle is however slower than SGD as the variance in the direction of negative eigenvalues, e.g. the escape directions, is smaller." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Normalized SAM = USAM with a time-dependent radius (exact): "while USAM has a fixed radius ρ, SAM has an time-dependent radius ρ/∥HXt∥ which is smaller than ρ if the dynamics is far from the origin (∥HXt∥ > 1) and larger when it is close to it (∥HXt∥ < 1)." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Lemma 4.3 (Lemma C.6, SAM ODE, exact): "For all ρ > 0, if H is PSD (Positive Semi-Definite), the origin is (locally) asymptotically stable. Additionally, if H is not PSD and ∥HXt∥ ≤ −ρλ∗, then the origin is still (locally) asymptotically stable." Interpretation in the paper: "for any value of ρ, if the dynamics of SAM is close enough to any critical point, i.e. enters an attractor, it is attracted by it. We also observe that if ρ → 0, this attractor reduces to a single point". - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- DNSAM SDE, Observation 4.4: "for all ρ > 0, there exists an ϵ > 0 such that if ∥HXt∥ ∈ (ϵ, −ρλ∗), the dynamics of Xt is attracted towards the origin. [...] On the contrary, if ∥HXt∥ < ϵ, then the dynamics is pushed away from the origin." (volatility, scaled by 1/||grad||, explodes very close to the critical point). - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Implicit regularizer and rho: f~_SAM(x) = f(x) + rho*E[||grad f_gamma(x)||_2]; "larger values of ρ increase the variance of the process. On the other hand, they also increase the marginal importance of the factor ρ^2∥∇f(x)∥^2 (USAM) and ρ∥∇f(x)∥ (DNSAM), and ρE[∥∇fγ(x)∥] (SAM), which for sufficiently large ρ might overshadow the marginal relevance of minimizing f and thus implicitly bias the optimizer toward any point with zero gradients, including saddles and maxima." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Contribution summary: "USAM might be attracted by saddles if ρ is too large. Differently, for any ρ > 0, DNSAM and SAM might be attracted by saddles but eventually, escape them after a long time." Conclusion: "we proved that the flow of USAM gets stuck around saddles if ρ is too large. In contrast, SAM oscillates around saddles if initialized close to them but eventually slowly escapes them thanks to the additional noise." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Linear autoencoder (Kunin et al. saddle at origin), dependence on initial distance sigma: "when SAM starts sufficiently far from it (σ ≥ 0.005), it optimizes immediately, while the closer it is initialized to it, the more it stays around it, up to not being able to move at all (σ ≤ 0.001)." Also: "full-batch SAM is attracted by the saddle while the others are able to escape it." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Scope limits: "most of our discussions are focused on the case where ρ = O(√η), which is not the only interesting setup"; SDE models "cannot fully capture the regime of large learning rates." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- On Rangwani et al. (see Q4): "(Rangwani et al., 2022) showed that USAM could in some cases escape saddles faster than SGD. We however note that their analysis is not completely formal [...] we here provide a more complete and rigorous description that shows that USAM can be much slower than SGD at escaping a saddle." - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)

### Inferences
- Lemma 4.3 with ||H X_t|| = ||grad f|| on a quadratic is literally the user's condition ||g|| < rho*mu, with mu = |lambda*| the weakest escape curvature (the binding one).
- The normalized-vs-unnormalized distinction cuts both ways: USAM is trapped only when rho is too large in absolute terms (rho > 1/mu), while normalized SAM (and ASAM, which also divides by a gradient norm) is trapped whenever the gradient is small enough, for any rho. A small-gradient plateau is exactly where normalization makes the effective radius largest.
- The implicit-regularizer statement gives a plain-language reading of the ln2 plateau: with a large rho, "keep ||grad|| small" can outweigh "lower the loss", and a near-constant predictor is a low-gradient region.

### Gaps
- No result covers ASAM's per-coordinate normalization, Adam as base optimizer, or cross-entropy specifically. Their SDE regime rho = O(sqrt(lr)) is far from rho = 0.5 with fine-tuning learning rates (inference about scale only; units differ for ASAM).

## Q3. Is the project's 1-D derivation (sign flip when ||g|| < rho*mu) consistent with the literature?

### Takeaway
Yes. The derivation reproduces Monzio Compagnoni et al.'s Lemma 4.3 condition and Kim et al.'s Theorem 1 condition with rho replaced by rho/||g||. Four caveats matter for this project: ||g|| is the full mini-batch norm, the second-order (Taylor) model breaks down for large rho, ASAM replaces rho by a weight-dependent radius, and the sign flip survives Adam's positive preconditioning. Status of the comparison: inference built on VERIFIED sources.

### Cited Findings
- Derivation as stated: on the escape direction f = -(mu/2)x^2, so g_x = -mu*x; normalized SAM uses eps = rho*g/||g||, giving grad f(w+eps)_x = -mu*x + rho*mu^2*x/||g||; the step is x <- x*(1 + lr*mu*(1 - rho*mu/||g||)), which pulls x back to 0 when ||g|| < rho*mu. Same algebra as Kim's eq. (10) lambda + rho~*lambda^2 > 0 with rho~ = rho/||grad|| - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2); and as Compagnoni's "if H is not PSD and ∥HXt∥ ≤ −ρλ∗, then the origin is still (locally) asymptotically stable" - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3)
- Large-rho breakdown of the quadratic picture: SAM can also stall where grad f(x+) = 0 although grad f(x) is not zero ("hallucinated minimizers"); a sufficient local-attraction condition is "ρ∥∇²f(xh)∥ < ∥∇f(xh)∥" together with isolation of the set. "Hallucinated minimizers represent a qualitatively different failure mode: saddle trapping corresponds to ∇f(x) = 0 (stationarity of the original loss), whereas hallucinated minimizers satisfy ∇f(x) ≠ 0 but ∇f(x+) = 0" - [Park, Jang, Ryu, Yang 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21818v2)
- ASAM's ascent step for p = 2 is eps_t = rho * T_w^2 grad L / ||T_w grad L||_2 with element-wise T_w = diag(|w_1|, ..., |w_k|), used as T_w + eta*I "for stability" - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Kim et al.'s Theorem 3 says smaller batches increase escape (more diffusion) - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2)

### Inferences
- Mini-batch norm: in practice ||g|| is the norm of a mini-batch gradient over all directions. Mini-batch noise and gradient components in non-escape directions both enlarge ||g|| and so shrink rho/||g||. This is the same direction as Kim's "smaller batch helps" and Compagnoni's volatility-driven escape, and it predicts that the flip is most likely exactly when the whole gradient (not just the escape component) is small, i.e. on a genuine plateau.
- ASAM version: replacing eps by rho*T^2 g/||T g|| makes the effective radius along a coordinate-aligned escape direction i about rho*t_i^2/||T g|| with t_i = |w_i| + eta, so the flip condition becomes roughly ||T_w g|| < rho*mu*t_i^2. Two consequences: directions living in large-magnitude pretrained weights are more prone to flip, and eta sets a floor on t_i for near-zero weights (a freshly initialized head). The exact multi-dimensional form depends on how escape directions align with coordinates; this has not been derived in any paper found.
- Adam: AdamW/RecAdam multiply the gradient by a positive per-coordinate factor (to first order, ignoring moment averaging), so the sign flip of the SAM gradient carries over to the update direction. For the same reason a global rescaling of the SAM gradient (SSAM, Q5) cannot undo the flip under Adam.
- Cheap diagnostic suggested by the two mechanisms (inference, using Park et al.'s diagnostics): on a plateau batch, log ||grad f(w)||, ||grad f(w+eps)||, f(w+eps) - f(w), and cos(grad f(w), grad f(w+eps)). Saddle trapping predicts ||grad f(w)|| small and cos < 0 along the escape direction; a hallucinated minimizer predicts ||grad f(w+eps)|| much smaller than ||grad f(w)|| and possibly f(w+eps) < f(w).

### Gaps
- No paper derives the trapping condition for ASAM, for Adam-based SAM, or for a cross-entropy constant-predictor plateau. Whether the ln2 plateau of this model is a true saddle (grad near 0) or a non-stationary low-gradient region has not been measured.

## Q4. Other theory relevant to early training, normalization of the ascent step, or rho size

### Takeaway
The broader theory splits into (a) results near minima (Bartlett-Long-Bousquet, Wen-Ma-Li, Dai-Ahn-Sra), where normalization stabilizes SAM, and (b) results far from minima (Kim, Compagnoni, Park et al., Agarwala-Dauphin, Kaddour), where large or normalized radii create attractors at saddles or at non-stationary points. Andriushchenko and Flammarion show the early-training SAM phase contributes little to final generalization. One NeurIPS paper (Rangwani et al.) argues the opposite direction, that high-rho SAM escapes saddles; its formality is disputed.

  - Switching experiments: "Interestingly, we observe that a method that is used at the beginning of training has little influence on the final performance. E.g., when SAM is switched to ERM within the first 70% epochs on CIFAR-100, the resulting model generalizes as well as ERM." Figure 9 caption: "we can see that SAM can gradually escape the worse-generalizing minimum found by ERM." Abstract: "fine-tuning a standard model with SAM can lead to significant generalization improvements". - [Andriushchenko & Flammarion 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06232v1)

However, our work reveals that SAM provides a strong regularization of the eigenvalues throughout the learning trajectory"; "Near initialization, full batch SAM provides limited suppression of large eigenvalues (Theorem 2.1)." Concerns sharpness control, not plateaus. - [Agarwala & Dauphin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.08692)
- Kaddour, Liu, Silva, Kusner, "When Do Flat Minima Optimizers Work?", NeurIPS 2022 (PDF footer "36th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2022)"), arXiv:2202.00661. VERIFIED - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00661v5)
  - Empirical saddle observation (GIN model on OGB): "SAM Obs. 3: SAM finds a saddle point. [...] A common pathology among curvature-based methods is that they attract saddle points [16]. Since SAM takes some form of curvature into account, too, we believe that future work should investigate SAM's propensity to find saddle points and potential remedies." - [Kaddour et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00661v5)
- Conflicting view - Rangwani, Aithal, Mishra, Babu, "Escaping Saddle Points for Effective Generalization on Class-Imbalanced Data", NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2212.13827. VERIFIED - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13827)
  - "As we find that as SAM with high ρ enhances the component of SGD in direction of negative curvature (γ) by (1 + ρλmin)^2, it is reasonable to expect that SAM is able to escape saddle points effectively and converge to solutions with significant less negative curvature quickly"; also "a high ρ is necessary for escaping saddle points and achieving the best results" (tail-class loss landscapes). Disputed by [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3) as "not completely formal".

### Inferences
- Rangwani's amplification factor (1 + rho*lambda_min)^2 exceeds 1 only when rho*|lambda_min| > 2, which lies inside the region rho*|lambda| > 1 where Compagnoni's USAM drift is already attracting. The two claims are therefore not directly contradictory: noise along the escape direction can grow while the deterministic drift points back to the saddle. Their evidence is also from late training on minority-class losses with SGD, not from step 1.
- The Dai-Ahn-Sra benefit of normalization (stability near minima) and the Kim/Compagnoni cost of normalization (effective radius blowing up near zero-gradient points) are two faces of dividing by ||grad||. Early in fine-tuning on a plateau, only the cost applies.

### Gaps
- Möllenhoff and Khan, "SAM as an Optimal Relaxation of Bayes" (ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.01620) was located but not read; no claim is made about it.
- No theory found that treats SAM on a cross-entropy constant-predictor plateau specifically.

## Q5. Practical fixes the literature proposes (rho warmup/schedule, delayed or late-phase SAM, smaller rho, renormalization, momentum/batch)

### Takeaway
The best-supported fix for step-1 trapping is to delay SAM: run the base optimizer first (Park et al. use a fixed 10% warm-start; Momentum-SAM sets rho = 0 during LR warmup; Zhou et al. and Andriushchenko-Flammarion show late-only SAM keeps nearly all of SAM's benefit). Next come a smaller rho, a rho schedule tied to the learning rate (GSAM), more momentum or smaller batches (Kim et al.), and gradient renormalization (SSAM). No paper found tests these fixes in transformer fine-tuning with ASAM.

### Cited Findings
- SGD warm-start (Park et al. 2026): "we run the base optimizer (SGD) for a short initial phase, and then enable SAM for the remainder of training. We use a fixed warm-start fraction 10% in all experiments and do not tune it." Rationale: "Early in training, iterates typically lie in high-loss regions, where encountering these basins is more likely. A brief SGD warm-start moves the trajectory away from high-loss regions before the SAM perturbation is enabled, reducing the chance of entering a hallucinated-minimizer basin without requiring ρ to be small." On the alternative: "A trivial way to reduce the risk of hallucinated minimizers is to keep ρ very small, but this can weaken the sharpness-regularization effect". CIFAR-100 / ResNet-18 (5 seeds, rho 0.1 to 0.8): both peak at rho = 0.3, "77.05% for SAM-only and 77.49% for the switching strategy". - [Park et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21818v2)
- Late-phase SAM: Zhou, Wang, Mao, Li, Yan, "Sharpness-Aware Minimization Efficiently Selects Flatter Minima Late in Training", ICLR 2025 (Spotlight), arXiv:2410.10373. VERIFIED - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10373v2). Quotes: "even a few epochs of SAM applied at the end of training yield nearly the same generalization and solution sharpness as full SAM training"; "applying SAM during the early phase offers only marginal benefits for generalization and sharpness compared to full SGD training"; "This suggests a fine-tuning scheme for SAM: start from a pretrained checkpoint and continue training with SAM for several epochs". Mechanism after a late switch: "i) SAM first escapes the minimum found by SGD exponentially fast; and ii) then rapidly converges to a flatter minimum within the same valley." Experiments are CIFAR-style SGD training from scratch.
- ERM to SAM switching: [Andriushchenko & Flammarion 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06232v1) (quotes in Q4).
- rho = 0 during warmup: Becker, Altrock, Risse, "Momentum-SAM: Sharpness Aware Minimization without Computational Overhead", arXiv:2401.12033 (v3 2 Oct 2025; preprint, venue not found). VERIFIED text - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12033). "During the learning rate warm-up phase commonly used for ViTs we set ρMSAM = 0." With MSAM active in warmup "we observe a drop in test accuracy from 69.1% to 66.1% which is below the AdamW baseline [...] A similar effect can be seen for SAM, however, it is less pronounced". Recommendation: "we would generally propose to apply SAM only after the warmup phase for ViT models to further improve SAM." On too-large rho for ViTs: "If chosen even higher, heavy instabilities occur during training, up to models not converging"; possible remedies listed as "clipping ϵ or scheduling of ρ, which we intend to pursue in future work."
- rho tied to the LR schedule: Zhuang et al., "Surrogate Gap Minimization Improves Sharpness-Aware Training" (GSAM), ICLR 2022, arXiv:2203.08065. VERIFIED - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.08065). Algorithm 1: rho_t = rho_min + (rho_max - rho_min)(lr - lr_min)/(lr_max - lr_min). Motivation is convergence theory: "Note that Thm. 5.1 assumes ρt to decay with t in order to prove the convergence, while SAM uses a constant ρ during training"; ablation: "Without ascent step, constant ρ and decayed ρt achieve similar performance." Search ranges: ResNets rho 0.01 to 0.05, ViTs and Mixers 0.05 to 0.6.
- Progressive increase / SAM late with large rho: "we found that progressively increasing the ρ value gives the best results [...] on ImageNet-LT, we use a large ρ of 0.5 in the last 30 epochs of training"; recommendation "starting with ρ = 0.05 and increasing ρ till the overall accuracy starts to decrease." - [Rangwani et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13827)
- Renormalization (SSAM): Tan, Zhang, Liu, Wang, Hao, "Stabilizing Sharpness-Aware Minimization Through A Simple Renormalization Strategy", JMLR 26(68):1-35, 2025, arXiv:2401.07250. VERIFIED - [JMLR page](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/24-0065.html); [arXiv v2 PDF read](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.07250v2). Abstract: SAM "is more prone to getting stuck at the saddle points [...] we propose a simple renormalization strategy, dubbed Stable SAM (SSAM), so that the gradient norm of the descent step maintains the same as that of the ascent step." Algorithm 1 line 6: g_asc <- (||g_t||/||g_asc||) g_asc. Linear autoencoder (d = 20, init N(0, 1e-4), lr 1e-3, rho in {0.02, 0.05, 0.08}): "both SAM and SAM∗ indeed require more time than SGD to escape from this point and become slower and slower as we gradually increase ρ up to not being able to escape anymore." Note their "SAM" is the unnormalized version (footnote 1) and SAM* is Foret's normalized one.
- Phase-restricted SAM variants cited by Tan et al. (not read, UNVERIFIED): "SALA (Tan et al., 2024) uses SAM only at the terminal phase of training when the distance between two consecutive steps is smaller than a threshold"; "AESAM (Jiang et al., 2023) designs an adaptive policy to apply SAM update only in the sharp regions"; LookSAM "runs a SAM update every few iterations"; RST switches SAM and standard training "with a scheduled probability". - [Tan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.07250v2)
- Momentum and batch size: [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2) Theorem 3 (Q1).
- Unnormalized SAM avoids the 1/||grad|| blowup but is trapped when rho > -1/lambda* and is less stable: [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3); [Dai, Ahn, Sra 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15287v2)

### Inferences
- Under Adam, SSAM's renormalization is a positive global rescale of the SAM gradient, which does not change its direction and to first order barely changes Adam's normalized step. SSAM is therefore unlikely to cure a sign-flip trap in an AdamW/RecAdam setup, unlike delaying SAM, which removes the trap condition by moving away from the small-gradient region before SAM starts.
- GSAM's formula ties rho_t to the LR; with the project's linear warmup (10% of steps), rho would be near rho_min during warmup and then decay with the LR. This is an implicit "rho warmup plus decay". GSAM did not test it as an anti-trapping device.
- Park et al.'s 10% fraction corresponds to 3 of 30 epochs in this project, the same length as the existing LR warmup. Momentum-SAM's rule ("rho = 0 during warmup") would give the same switch point.

### Gaps
- No source tests delayed SAM, rho warmup, or SSAM with ASAM in transformer fine-tuning. LookSAM, ESAM, and SALA original papers were not read; nothing found suggests they target early-training instability (LookSAM and ESAM are framed as compute savings in the citing text).
- The search turned up "SAWD" (SAM switched on only in the decay phase of a warmup-stable-decay schedule), but its primary source was not identified or read; treat it as UNVERIFIED.

## Q6. Are there reports of SAM/ASAM causing training failure or a slow start in transformer fine-tuning?

### Takeaway
No direct report was found of SAM or ASAM pinning a fine-tuned transformer classifier at a constant predictor. Fine-tuning papers (T5/mT5, RoBERTa, BERT) report gains with small SAM radii (0.01 to 0.2, Foret units). The closest documented failures are: ViT accuracy dropping when (Momentum-)SAM is on during warmup, ViT runs "not converging" at too-high rho, VGG19-BN runs collapsing to chance in the ASAM paper itself, and plateaus at positive loss for large rho in Park et al.

### Cited Findings
- Bahri, Mobahi, Tay, "Sharpness-Aware Minimization Improves Language Model Generalization", ACL 2022 (arXiv comment), arXiv:2110.08529. VERIFIED - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08529v2). "We show that blithely applying SAM when fine-tuning public pre-trained checkpoints of the text-to-text transformer (T5) [...] improves test performance quite markedly." Algorithm 1 default "neighborhood size ρ > 0 (default 0.15)"; tables use SAM (0.02), (0.05), (0.15).
- Kaddour et al. 2022: RoBERTa-base GLUE fine-tuning with "ρ ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2}, as in previous work"; no failure reported for these. - [Kaddour et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00661v5)
- Zhong et al., "Improving Sharpness-Aware Minimization with Fisher Mask for Better Generalization on Language Models" (FSAM), Findings of EMNLP 2022 per ACL Anthology search result, arXiv:2210.05497. PARTIAL (text read; venue from search result only) - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.05497). On sub-sampled GLUE (10% to 90%): "We can observe consistent gains from both SAM and our FSAM across all sizes of sub-sampled training sets". No failure reported.
- ViT warmup: "drop in test accuracy from 69.1% to 66.1%" with MSAM during warmup, and "heavy instabilities occur during training, up to models not converging" at too-high rho. - [Becker et al., Momentum-SAM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12033)
- ASAM paper footnotes for VGG19-BN: "Some runs completely failed, thus giving 10% of accuracy (success rate: SGD: 3/5, SAM 1/5, ASAM 3/5)" (appears to belong to the CIFAR-10 table) and "(success rate: SGD: 5/5, SAM 4/5, ASAM 4/5)" (appears to belong to the CIFAR-100 table). PARTIAL for the table assignment (two-column extraction) - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Park et al.: SAM-only "frequently plateaus at positive loss and suffers accuracy collapse" at larger rho (MNIST, full batch), and on CIFAR-100 "SAM-only training becomes increasingly unstable as ρ grows". - [Park et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21818v2)

### Inferences
- The fine-tuning literature that reports success uses Foret-style SAM with rho of at most 0.2 (absolute units) and large datasets (GLUE, SuperGLUE, TyDiQA). This project uses ASAM rho = 0.5 (weight-relative units) on 456 functions. The literature therefore does not contradict the observed failure; it simply does not test this regime.
- Project context (not literature): CLAUDE.md section 7 already records that SAM rho = 0.05 in Phase 1 pinned CodeBERT at ln2 for 13 epochs (F1 0.3333). That is the same failure signature with plain SAM.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on SAM/ASAM with CodeBERT or code models, or with datasets of a few hundred examples. Search coverage was limited (2 targeted web searches plus citation chasing).

## Q7. ASAM paper (Kwon et al., ICML 2021): recommended rho for ASAM vs SAM, whether rho = 0.5 is typical, and what eta does

### Takeaway
rho = 0.5 with eta = 0.01 is the ASAM paper's CIFAR-10 setting (SGD, lr 0.1, cosine, 200 epochs, from scratch); CIFAR-100 used rho = 1.0. For a Transformer trained with Adam (IWSLT'14), grid search picked rho = 0.2 for ASAM (0.1 for SAM). eta is a small additive term in the normalization operator (T_w + eta*I) "for stability", trading adaptivity for stability. ASAM's rho is measured in weight-relative units, so it is not comparable to SAM's rho. Status: VERIFIED.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: Jungmin Kwon, Jeongseop Kim, Hyunseo Park, In Kwon Choi, "ASAM: Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization for Scale-Invariant Learning of Deep Neural Networks", ICML 2021, PMLR 139:5905-5914. arXiv:2102.11600 (v3 29 Jun 2021). VERIFIED - [PMLR page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kwon21b.html); [arXiv v3 PDF read](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Normalization operator: element-wise "Tw = diag(|w1|, . . . , |wk|)"; "Note that we use Tw + ηIk rather than Tw for sufficiently small η > 0 for stability. η is a hyper-parameter controlling trade-off between adaptivity and stability." - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Ascent step for p = 2: eps_t = rho * T_wt^2 grad L(w_t) / ||T_wt grad L(w_t)||_2 (Algorithm 1). - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- CIFAR settings (exact): "we first conduct a grid search over {0.00005, 0.0001, 0.0002, . . . , 0.5, 1.0, 2.0} for finding appropriate values of ρ. We use ρ = 0.5 for CIFAR-10 and ρ = 1.0 for CIFAR-100, because it gives moderately good performance across various models. We set ρ for SAM as 0.05 for CIFAR-10 and 0.1 for CIFAR-100 as in Foret et al. (2021). η for ASAM is set to 0.01." Other settings: batch 128, 200 epochs for SAM/ASAM, momentum 0.9, weight decay 5e-4, cosine LR from 0.1, label smoothing 0.1. - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Transformer + Adam (IWSLT'14 DE-EN; Adam lr 0.0005, betas 0.9/0.98, dropout 0.3, weight decay 1e-4, label smoothing 0.1): "We choose ρ = 0.1 for SAM and ρ = 0.2 for ASAM as a result of a grid search over {0.005, 0.01, 0.02, . . . , 0.5, 1.0, 2.0} using validation dataset." - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Toy example on rho range: "If w0 = (0.3, 0.033), while SAM even fails to converge to the valley with ρ = 0.05, ASAM converges no matter which w0 is used if ρ < √2. In other words, appropriate ρ for SAM is dependent on the scales of w on the training trajectory, whereas ρ of ASAM is not." - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)
- Failed runs footnotes for VGG19-BN: see Q6. - [Kwon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600v3)

### Inferences
- The project's (rho = 0.5, eta = 0.01) is the from-scratch, SGD, CIFAR-10 recipe, not the paper's Adam/Transformer recipe (rho = 0.2), and not a fine-tuning recipe; the paper contains no fine-tuning experiment.
- Because ||T_w^-1 eps||_2 = rho, the perturbation is about rho times each weight's own magnitude in the directions where |w_i * g_i| is large. With rho = 0.5, the chosen directions can be shifted by up to about half of their pretrained magnitude at each ascent step. eta only matters for weights much smaller than 0.01 (for example zero-initialized biases or a fresh head), where it sets the perturbation floor.

### Gaps
- The paper gives no guidance on rho for pretrained-model fine-tuning or small datasets, and no ablation on eta.

## Q8. How well does the literature explain the measured MultiVD pattern?

### Takeaway
Every element of the pattern has a matching mechanism in VERIFIED sources: one-sided lengthening of the plateau (Kim Cor. 1; Compagnoni Lemma 4.1/4.3; Tan Fig. 1), cure by turning SAM on late (Park et al.; Zhou et al.; Andriushchenko-Flammarion; Momentum-SAM), no learn-then-collapse (attraction is local to the low-gradient start region), rare late noise-driven escape (Compagnoni), and permanent trapping when started closest to the saddle (Compagnoni's autoencoder; Kim's "permanent trapping"). The literature does not decide whether the ln2 plateau is a true saddle or a hallucinated minimizer; that is measurable (Q3 diagnostic).

### Cited Findings
- (a) ASAM lengthens the plateau in 33/35 cells without RecAdam and 24/30 with RecAdam, never shortens it (49/49 and 64/64 in older blocks). Predicted direction: "SAM escapes saddle points more slowly than SGD", with the gap proportional to rho - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2); "USAM will escape the saddle but more slowly than the gradient flow" and SAM attraction whenever ||grad|| <= rho*|lambda*| - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3); "slower and slower as we gradually increase ρ up to not being able to escape anymore" - [Tan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.07250v2). Opposing claim for late, tail-class SGD: [Rangwani et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13827).
- (b) Turning ASAM on late removed the plateau in 15/15 cells. Matches: "switching remains stable across ρ" - [Park et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21818v2); late-only SAM keeps the benefit - [Zhou et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10373v2), [Andriushchenko & Flammarion 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06232v1); rho = 0 during warmup - [Becker et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12033).
- (c) 0/165 cells learned and then collapsed. Matches the local nature of the attraction results: trapping happens near a critical (or hallucinated) point reached early; near minima normalized SAM is stable ("if H is PSD [...] the origin is (locally) asymptotically stable") - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3), [Dai, Ahn, Sra 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15287v2). Park et al. also locate the risk "Early in training [...] in high-loss regions" - [Park et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21818v2).
- (d) Without early stopping (min_epochs 30): one cell escaped at epoch 18 (test ROC 0.929); two stayed at ln2 for 30 epochs, both from Phase-1 checkpoints chosen while Phase 1 was itself on a plateau. Matches "SAM oscillates around saddles if initialized close to them but eventually slowly escapes them thanks to the additional noise", and the autoencoder result that closer initializations stay longer, "up to not being able to move at all (σ ≤ 0.001)" - [Monzio Compagnoni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08203v3); "permanent trapping in saddle regions can become a failure mode" - [Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06308v2).
- (e) 1-D derivation: consistent with Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 1 (see Q3).

### Inferences
- (a) The one-sidedness ("never shortens") is the most diagnostic part. Kim's Cor. 1 difference is positive for every rho > 0, so the theory predicts lengthening or no change and never shortening. A positive Rangwani-type effect would have produced at least some shortened cells.
- (a) The weaker effect with RecAdam (24/30 vs 33/35) is not addressed by any source. A hypothesis only: RecAdam's pull toward the pretrained weights adds positive curvature and changes the early effective step, which could move cells out of the flip regime. Untested.
- (b) A plausible mechanism for the late-switch cure is that by the switch point ||g|| (or the distance from the plateau) is large enough that ||g|| < rho*mu no longer holds. This fits Park et al.'s "away from high-loss regions" rationale. Caveat: Zhou et al. and Andriushchenko-Flammarion measure generalization after a late switch, not plateau avoidance, and use SGD from scratch.
- (d) With the linear LR decaying to 0, the diffusion (escape) term in Kim's SDE scales with the LR, while the attraction condition in the ODE does not depend on it. The chance of a noise-driven escape should therefore fall over the run. Epoch 18 of 30 (with 3 warmup epochs) corresponds to roughly 44% of peak LR. This favours early-stopping patience longer than 8 epochs only if the goal is to measure trapping, not to rescue the run.
- (d) The two permanently trapped cells starting from plateau-selected Phase-1 checkpoints are the direct analogue of Compagnoni's small-sigma initializations. This is a reason to log the Phase-1 checkpoint's own gradient norm and loss next to each Phase-2 cell.
- Distinguishing mechanisms: if the ln2 plateau has non-negligible ||grad f(w)|| but tiny ||grad f(w+eps)||, Park et al.'s hallucinated-minimizer account fits better than saddle trapping; if ||grad f(w)|| itself is tiny and cos(grad f(w), grad f(w+eps)) < 0, the Kim/Compagnoni sign-flip account fits. Train loss 0.70 being slightly above ln2 = 0.693 does not by itself decide between them.

### Gaps
- All cited theory is for SGD-type or full-batch dynamics on smooth losses; none covers ASAM + AdamW/RecAdam fine-tuning of a pretrained transformer. The match to the measured pattern is qualitative.
- Whether the plateau is a saddle, a hallucinated minimizer, or simply a low-gradient region has not been measured in this project.
